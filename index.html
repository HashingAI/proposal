What it is meant to change

The system exists to serve four operating goals. Each phase is sequenced to move these in the right direction, and Phase 4 exists so C2K can actually see whether they moved.

A standard workflow, every time

Intake, test, shelve, list, sell, ship: the same steps in the same order for staff and volunteers alike, with required fields so no unit slips through half-finished.

Nothing lost, nothing sold twice

Every device has one permanent record and one known shelf. Scan-verified picking is designed to end wrong-unit shipments, and cross-channel sync closes the double-sale gap.

Faster turnover

Items that are findable and quick to list reach the market while they still hold value. Cutting the days between intake and listing is the single biggest lever on turn rate.

More revenue per staff hour

The same three-person team handling more units, on more channels, with a commission-free store keeping more of every sale for the mission.

Where to start

Phase 1 is the decision that matters now. It is the foundation the other three read from, and it is the only phase where waiting has a cost: dashboards, automation, and the store can all be added later, but the history they analyze can only be captured from the day tracking begins. Data not recorded in Phase 1 cannot be reconstructed.

Phase 3 is worth deciding at the same time, since building it alongside Phase 1 is what makes the $6,000 price possible and lets both launch together. Phases 2 and 4 can wait for their moment without losing anything.
