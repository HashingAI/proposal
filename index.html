What the system changes
A standard workflow, every time

Intake, test, shelve, list, sell, ship: the same steps in the same order for staff and volunteers alike, with required fields so no unit slips through half-finished.

Nothing lost, nothing sold twice

Every device has one permanent record and one known shelf. Scan-verified picking ends wrong-unit shipments, and cross-channel sync closes the double-sale gap.

Faster turnover

Items that are findable and quick to list reach the market while they still hold value. Cutting the days between intake and listing is the single biggest lever on revenue.

More revenue per staff hour

The same team handles more units on more channels, with a commission-free store keeping more of every sale for the mission instead of eBay fees.

Full traceability on every device

Who donated it, who tested it, where it was shelved, when it sold, and who shipped it — a complete timestamped record per unit, permanently. Nothing to reconstruct after the fact.

Leadership visibility to set goals and act

Turn rate, aging inventory, channel profitability, and sales trends — all computed automatically from the same records staff create by working. Numbers to plan from, not spreadsheets to maintain.

Where to start

Phase 1 is the decision that matters now. It is the foundation the other three read from, and it is the only phase where waiting has a cost: dashboards, automation, and the store can all be added later, but the history they analyze can only be captured from the day tracking begins. Data not recorded in Phase 1 cannot be reconstructed.

Phase 3 is worth deciding at the same time, since building it alongside Phase 1 is what makes the $6,000 price possible and lets both launch together. Phases 2 and 4 can wait for their moment without losing anything.
